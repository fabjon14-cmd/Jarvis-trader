## Rule adherence

Re-reading the Trading Session entries across the full window against
CLAUDE.md's rules:

- **No repeat of the AAPL earnings-window violation.** Every subsequent
  new-buy decision explicitly checked `earnings`/`within_48h` before acting
  (e.g., CAT/AMD blocked pre-earnings 8/3–8/4; PFE/DIS/CVX blocked 7/31–8/4).
  The 7/29 position itself remained the account's biggest drag all window but
  was only ever traded again for an unrelated, legitimate catalyst (the 8/10
  Jefferies downgrade trim).
- **Falling-knife / stabilization rule: no violations, arguably applied more
  conservatively than necessary.** ORCL was repeatedly denied an add for 6+
  consecutive days (8/6–8/13) purely on the strict 2-completed-close test,
  even while sitting on double-digit unrealized gains — a defensible,
  rule-compliant call, but worth the operator's attention: the rule is
  currently indistinguishable between "falling knife" and "healthy uptrend
  with one soft day," and cost the account upside on its best performer.
- **No averaging-down violations** — every position with a negative mark
  (AAPL, and BAC/PG/TSLA whenever briefly negative) was correctly blocked
  from adds on every check.
- **Sector cap and 5-position cap correctly enforced throughout** —
  Information Technology sat at 2/2 (AAPL, ORCL) from 7/31 onward, blocking
  NVDA/MSFT/AMD/CRM/ADBE adds; the 5-position cap was full from 8/3 onward
  almost continuously, both self-enforced pre-submission with no rejected
  orders needed to catch a breach.
- **Stop-loss and circuit breaker: never triggered, both correctly checked
  every run.** No position ever crossed -15%; portfolio-level intraday/5-day
  drawdown stayed within roughly ±0.03%–0.12% throughout, nowhere near the
  -4%/-8% halt thresholds.
- **Selling-rule interpretation was inconsistent within a single day
  (2026-08-07).** The 10:17 ET firing read the 7/30 HSBC PG downgrade as
  *not* clearing the "clearly negative" bar because HSBC's price target was
  still above market — a substance test CLAUDE.md's rule doesn't specify. The
  12:12 ET firing, reacting to a second (Argus) downgrade, explicitly
  abandoned that self-invented test in favor of the rule's plain wording
  ("a downgrade" is listed with no target-price carve-out) and trimmed PG.
  The eventual action was rule-compliant, but the same catalyst-type was
  read two different ways in one session — worth tightening the "clearly
  negative" definition so it doesn't depend on which firing evaluates it.
- **Borderline: the same catalyst was used to justify two separate PG
  sells six days apart.** The 8/13 trim cited the identical 8/7 Argus
  downgrade as its "clearly negative" catalyst — technically still within
  the 5-trading-day staleness window on a 4-trading-day-old read, but using
  one downgrade to justify two distinct sell actions is a double-dip the
  rule doesn't explicitly forbid but likely wasn't intended to allow. Flag
  for clarification: should a catalyst that already drove one sell/trim be
  eligible to drive a second, later one on the same position?
- **5%-of-buying-power cap essentially untested this window** — position
  sizes stayed in the $50–$500 range against ~$396–400K buying power (a cap
  north of $19,800), so the real binding constraints throughout were the
  daily/weekly notional caps and the 5-position cap, not per-symbol sizing.
- **Watchlist scope respected** — all trading stayed within `watchlist.json`
  even as it expanded from 32 to 50 symbols on 8/5.
