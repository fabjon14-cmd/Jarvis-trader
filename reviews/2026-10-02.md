## Rule adherence

Re-reading this week's Trading Session journal entries (09-28 through 10-02) against the
Trading rules in CLAUDE.md:

- **Mechanical stop-loss cascade (the week's headline issue).** The stop-loss rule reads:
  "-15% from cost basis → trim the position to half its current size," checked and acted on
  "MUST... same run," every firing. Taken completely literally, this has no stated floor or
  cooldown, so when a position's price stays below -15% without recovering, trimming to half
  doesn't change the unrealized percentage on the residual — it re-triggers the very next
  firing, and the next, converging toward zero without ever reaching the -25% close-entirely
  threshold. This is exactly what happened: ORCL (7 trims in one day, 09-28, continuing a
  cascade that started 09-24) and BAC (17 filled trims across 09-30/10-01/10-02, plus 1
  canceled and 1 still-pending). **Every individual trim was, in isolation, the literal rule
  working correctly** — the journal entries explicitly cite the data point
  (`unrealized_plpc`, `action: trim_half`) behind every single one, so the audit-trail
  requirement was met for the trims themselves. But the cumulative outcome — a real position
  mechanically ground down to sub-cent dust over 3 trading days without ever cleanly exiting —
  is very unlikely to be what the operator intended when writing "trim to half," and it was
  flagged as exactly that by the routine itself, repeatedly, in real time (09-30, and every
  firing from 10-01 onward). This is a **rule-design gap, not a rule violation**, but it is now
  confirmed as a recurring, not one-off, problem (ORCL's instance predates this week; BAC's
  started and ran its full course within this week alone) and needs an explicit operator
  decision — e.g., a minimum-residual-value floor (close outright, rather than trim-half again,
  once the post-trim residual would fall under some small dollar amount or round below
  Alpaca's practical fractional-order minimum).
- **Double-trim process error (10-01, 14:16–14:18 ET).** At the first firing of the day, a
  stale unfilled BAC order from 09-30's close was found and canceled, and the stop-loss check
  (re-run on the reduced residual) then triggered a second, separate trim roughly 2 minutes
  later — two sell fills in one firing rather than the one mandatory action the rule
  contemplates per run. The journal self-identifies this as a process error, not a deliberate
  double-dip, and each individual trim was still correctly sized off the data at hand, but it
  resulted in more aggressive de-risking of BAC that day than the rule as written calls for.
  Flagging this explicitly since it compounds the cascade issue above.
- **Two audit-trail gaps (unjournaled fills).** Cross-checking Alpaca's own order history
  against the journal found two BAC sell fills that aren't recorded in any Trading Session
  entry: one submitted 10-01 20:16 ET / filled 10-02 13:30 UTC (the market-open fill of a
  previous day's pending order), and one submitted 10-02 09:23 ET / filled 10-02 09:30 UTC.
  Both reconcile cleanly against the known qty sequence (nothing is unaccounted-for
  financially — Alpaca's history is authoritative and both orders are legitimate), but neither
  shows up in that day's Trading Session narrative, which is a gap against the "every decision
  this run makes... gets logged" audit-trail requirement. This looks like a session that
  crashed or restarted between submitting an order and writing the journal entry for it — worth
  an infra-level look, not a trading-discipline problem.
